⚖️ SC JUDGMENT Jul 2026 · 5-judge bench HIGH PROB
K.S. Puttaswamy-II — Digital privacy is a fundamental right under Art. 21
A five-judge constitutional bench unanimously held that digital privacy is integral to Article 21. The bench applied the "doctrine of constitutional silence" — the absence of explicit textual mention does not extinguish a constitutional right. Covert data harvesting without consent violates Art. 21. Parliament directed to enact comprehensive data protection legislation within six months with an independent Data Protection Authority.
CLAT angle: LR — Art. 21, doctrine of constitutional silence. CA — Data Protection Authority, PDP Bill, Puttaswamy precedent.

⚖️ SC JUDGMENT Jun 2026 · 3-judge bench HIGH PROB
Anuj v. State of Bihar — EIAs must include community consultation; absolute liability near settlements
SC held that Environmental Impact Assessments must mandatorily include community consultation before industrial clearance. Public Trust Doctrine invoked — environment is held in trust for citizens, not for commercial exploitation. Absolute liability (MC Mehta) applies without exception near human settlements, unlike strict liability (Rylands v Fletcher) which permits force majeure defences. CPCB directed to set emission standards within 60 days.
CLAT angle: LR — Absolute vs strict liability, public trust doctrine, Art. 21 clean environment. CA — CPCB, EIA process.

📋 Before you start — CLAT 2024 key insights
English (24 Qs): 3 of 4 passages were fiction (Jhumpa Lahiri, Anita Desai, Shakespeare). Only 1 was legal/current. Focus on inference, tone, vocabulary in context. Mostly elimination-based.
Current Affairs (28 Qs): 2 static GK passages (General Dyer, Ambedkar), 3 current (Israel-Palestine, Chandrayaan-3, G20). Several Qs directly answerable from passage — read carefully first.
Legal Reasoning (32 Qs): Mix of one-liner and hypothetical Qs. 3 passages on new Indian Acts. All answerable from passage principles — no prior law knowledge needed.
Logical Reasoning (24 Qs): 2 long, 2 short passages. Mix of contextual and textual Qs. Some tricky — use elimination. AI and Kota suicides passages were more analytical.
Quant (12 Qs): Easiest section — takes just 7–8 min. 2 DI passages with tables and graphs. Attempt all 12 confidently. Don't spend more than 10 min here.
Strategy: Start with Quant (10 min) → CA (28 min) → Legal (35 min) → English (25 min) → Logic (22 min). Save 5 min for review. Target 100–108 attempts with 80%+ accuracy.

📰 Passage — Current Affairs & Legal Reasoning
The Supreme Court of India, in a landmark judgment delivered in June 2026, held that the right to access clean air constitutes a fundamental right under Article 21 of the Constitution. The bench, comprising five judges, was adjudicating a petition filed by residents of an industrial town who alleged that emissions from a nearby factory had severely degraded air quality, leading to widespread respiratory illness. The Court applied the principle of "absolute liability" as established in the MC Mehta case, holding that industries engaged in hazardous activities cannot escape liability even if they exercised all reasonable care. The judgment further directed the Central Pollution Control Board to frame binding emission standards within sixty days. The Court noted that the right to a healthy environment has been consistently recognised in international environmental law, including the Stockholm Declaration of 1972 and the Rio Declaration of 1992. The bench also distinguished this case from the Rylands v Fletcher rule of strict liability by emphasising that no exceptions apply when public health is directly threatened by industrial activity at large scale.
